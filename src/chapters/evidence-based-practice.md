# Chapter 9 Evidence Based Practice

## Introduction
Evidence-Based Practice (EBP) is a cornerstone in modern healthcare, guiding practitioners to make decisions grounded in the best available, current, valid, and relevant evidence. Dr. David Sackett, a pioneer in EBP, defined it as the "conscientious, explicit, and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about the care of the individual patient." This practice integrates individual clinical expertise with external clinical evidence, emphasizing the inclusion of patients' concerns, expectations, and values [Bowling, 2014, p.158]. The importance of EBP lies in its potential to reduce inconsistency in healthcare practices, enhance efficiency, and increase effectiveness.

## Why Evidence-Based Practice?
Promoting Inquiry and Critical Thinking: EBP instills an attitude of inquiry among health professionals. It encourages them to question and evaluate their practices, fostering continuous improvement and innovation.
Ensuring Effective Resource Utilization: Finite health resources are used wisely through EBP. This approach ensures that decisions about funding health services are informed by relevant evidence, maximizing the impact of available resources [Research Methods in Health].
Enhancing Quality of Care: By integrating the best available evidence, EBP contributes to the delivery of high-quality, patient-centered care. This is crucial in improving health outcomes and patient satisfaction.

### 1. ASK - Formulating an Answerable Clinical Question
Asking a specific and focused question is essential. The PICO framework (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) is widely used to structure questions. Other tools like PICOT, SPICE, and ECLIPSE cater to different research problems. For example, a question could be, "Do patients undergoing lumbar punctures need four hours' bed rest to minimize the risk of post-lumbar puncture headaches?".

### 2. ACQUIRE - Obtaining the Best Available Evidence
Not all research is equally valuable. Clinically useful research satisfies criteria such as addressing a real and important problem, adding substantially to existing knowledge, having a pragmatic design, measuring patient-relevant outcomes, providing good value for money, being feasible and acceptable in the real world, and making study data available for verification [How to Implement Evidence-Based Healthcare, p.15].

#### 2.1 Evidence Hierarchy:
The hierarchy of evidence is a system used in evidence-based practices to rank the relative strength of results obtained from scientific research. The diagram typically takes the shape of a pyramid, with the highest quality evidence at the top and the lowest at the bottom. Here's a general outline for a hierarchy of evidence diagram:

*   **Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses:** At the top of the pyramid. These studies synthesize data from multiple studies.
*   **Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs):** Below systematic reviews. These studies involve randomly allocating participants into experimental and control groups.
*   **Cohort Studies:** Observational studies where participants with a certain condition or treatment are followed over time and compared with a similar group who are not affected by the condition under investigation.
*   **Case-Control Studies:** Observational studies where two groups differing in outcome are identified and compared on the basis of some supposed causal attribute.
*   **Cross-Sectional Surveys/Studies:** Observational studies that analyze data from a population, or a representative subset, at a specific point in time.
*   **Case Reports and Series:** Reports on the treatment of individual patients or a report on a single patient.
*   **Expert Opinion:** At the bottom of the pyramid. Includes editorials, expert opinions, and commentary.

### Appraising the evidence
From: https://canberra.libguides.com/evidence
#### Is not all Published Research of Good Quality?
Not all research is of sufficient quality to inform clinical decision making. Therefore you need to critically appraise evidence before using it to inform your clinical decision making. The three major aspects of evidence that you need to critically appraise are:
*   **Validity** - can you trust it?
*   **Impact** - are the results clinically important?
*   **Applicability** - can you apply it to your patient?

Critical appraisal is a systematic process, a central process of evidence-based practice, which helps the reader to determine:
*   If the study presented in the research paper addresses a clear and focus question?
*   If the methods used to answer the question were valid
*   Are the results/ findings of the study valid, informative and important?
*   Can these findings be applied in a practice setting?

**Associated cost**
EBP has a cost implication as any new practice or a change in practice may require changes in how a service is delivered, and therefore, there are costs associated with it. The kind of questions that you may need to ask yourself include
*   What are the financial implications of the interventions proposes
*   How much money is organisation willing to spend on the intervention proposed
*   A clearly articulated and itemised budget detailing every aspect of the required cost related to human and material resources should be developed. You may need to think of staff cost, stationary, electronic or technological equipment, meeting arrangement and catering cost etc.

**Time**
*   Developing a timeline that specifies a time for various activities associated with the project should be developed.
*   Such timeline helps in ensuring on time completion of various activities associated with the project.

**Resources**
*   Identification of resources helps in the prior arrangement of resources and specification of financial requirements for the project

**Deciding on outcome measure**
*   It is important to identify and decide measures of success, which will help in identifying and determining the success of the change implemented. Both short term indicators (which can be achieved within 6 months) and long term indicators (which may be achieved in the longer period) need to be specified.
*   It is also essential to identify ways to collect data about indicators of success

**Identify support groups**
*   Change cannot be implemented without getting appropriate support and acceptance from the target audience. Therefore, it is important to identify people who like and support your idea and can support you in implementing change. These people can be champions, managers, those who have an interest in the subject etc

**Identify strategies to implement change**
*   Creating awareness and interest
*   Building knowledge and commitment
*   Promoting action and adaptation
*   Pursue integration and sustainability

### Assess/Evaluate the Process
Once implemented, changed or new practice should be evaluated to see if the practice is accepted, rejected or need some modification to suit the needs of a specific setting. Evaluation helps in exploring and comparing the effects of evaluation with baseline (before the implementation of new intervention) or with other areas of practice where intervention is not implemented. Evaluation may also provide useful information about the process of implementation and strategies that worked and those which did not work. It may also help in identification of staff training and development needs with regards to the new practice. Evaluation may also help identification and recognition or new issues that may need to be explored in the next cycle f EBP.

Evidence based practice is seen as comprising five explicit steps
1.  Identify a problem from practice and turn it into a specific question
2.  Find the best available evidence that relates to the specific question usually through a taro and systematic search of the literature
3.  Critically appraise the evidence validity usefulness and methodological rigour
4.  Identify and use the current best evidence and together with the patient or clients preferences and the practitioner's expertise and experience, apply it to the situation
5.  Evaluate the effect on the patient outlined and reflect on the nurses own performance

#### Audit
Audit is directed at the maintenance and achievement of quality in Healthcare.
Audit aims to improve patient outcome, to develop a more cost-effective use of resources and to have an educational function for health professionals.
Audit can be carried out internally by organizations, members of a discipline, individuals who systematically review their work or that of their teams, or external bodies. The process of audit involves multiple methods, such as document searching and analysis, analysis of retained data, clinical case reviews and presentation in team meetings. it can also include the collection of information by focus groups or patients or by questionnaire, for example, patient satisfaction, patient-assessed outcome.
